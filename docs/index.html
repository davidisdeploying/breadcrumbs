how it works

Three steps, about thirty seconds.

1

Open your orders

Sign in to your grocery store like always and go to your orders page. breadcrumbs notices the store on its own.

2

Gather them

Click Gather recent orders. It quietly opens each recent order and reads the items — names, quantities, prices.

3

Download the file

Click Bag it up to save a clean itemized file. Import it into your budgeting app, spreadsheet, or wherever you keep records.

your data stays yours

It only reads what's already on your screen.

Your login stays yours

It never asks for or stores your password. It works inside the store page you're already signed into — nothing more.

Nothing leaves your browser

The only thing that goes anywhere is the file you choose to save. No accounts, no servers, no tracking, no analytics.

Open source

Every line is on GitHub. It doesn't trick the site or automate logins — it just reads the orders you can already see.

install it

Up and running in two minutes.

breadcrumbs isn't on the Chrome Web Store yet — for now you load it straight from the source. It's quick, and it's the same thing developers do every day.

Download from GitHub Read the docs
Download the latest version from GitHub and unzip it somewhere you'll remember.
Open Chrome and go to chrome://extensions in the address bar.
Turn on Developer mode — the toggle in the top‑right corner.
Click Load unpacked (top‑left) and choose the unzipped breadcrumbs folder.
Pin the toast icon: click the puzzle‑piece in Chrome's toolbar, then the pin next to breadcrumbs.
Open your grocery store's orders page, click the toast → Gather recent orders → Bag it up.

Works in Chrome and other Chromium browsers (Edge, Brave, Arc). A Chrome Web Store listing is in the works.

questions

Good things to know.

What does breadcrumbs actually do?
When you're on your grocery store's order page, it reads the orders shown there and saves a file listing every item with its quantity and price. That's it — it doesn't buy anything, change anything, or place orders. It just turns “one charge” into “here's what was in it.”
What's the file, and what do I do with it?
It saves a breadcrumbs-orders.json — a small, plain text data file. You can open it in a spreadsheet, feed it to a budgeting tool, or just keep it for your records. It pairs especially well with budgeting apps like Monarch; support for exporting other formats (like CSV) is on the way.
Is it safe? Does it see my password?
No password, ever. breadcrumbs only works inside a store page you've already signed into yourself, in your own browser. Nothing is sent to any server — there are no accounts, no tracking, and no analytics. And because it's open source, you can read exactly what it does.
Which stores does it support?
Kroger and H‑E‑B today, with more on the way. Each store is a small add-on, so the list grows over time — if your store isn't here yet, it may be soon.
Which browsers does it work in?
Chrome and other Chromium-based browsers — Edge, Brave, and Arc. It's a desktop browser extension, so you'll gather your orders on a computer rather than a phone. A Safari version is planned.
Why isn't it on the Chrome Web Store?
A store listing is in the works. For now you install it from the source in about two minutes — the steps are right here.
Does it cost anything?
No. breadcrumbs is free and open source. There's nothing to buy and no subscription.
